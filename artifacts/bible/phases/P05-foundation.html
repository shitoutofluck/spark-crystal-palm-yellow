Utah habit — CIP / CMU basement

m-P05-basement-cip-cmu. Also called CIP basement on spread footing, CMU basement on spread footing, full basement. Cold-climate / East–Midwest habit (HUD RSDG §4.1; ORNL BFH §1.3). Wasatch + Logan regional habit (30 in frost). Fontana: not the Southern habit.

In cold country this is the usual basement: poured wall or block on a spread footing, bottom of footing below frost. Waterproof and put drain tile on before you backfill. Do not dump dirt against the walls until the first-floor framing is on to brace the top. CIP or block is whichever the local yard pours or lays.

When this is the buy: cold climates / East–Midwest and Wasatch/Logan (30 in frost). ORNL §1.3: MN-style frost depth makes half the wall already required. Walkout combo (basement uphill + slab downhill) is normal on slopes (ORNL §1.3) — same method, not a new type.

When this is a bad idea: veto if water table within ~8 ft (ORNL §1.3) — use crawl or slab. Not the Fontana / South-Southwest habit. Unreinforced wall limits exceeded (ORNL notes 14, 22) without engineer / IRC tables. Piles/helical are not this method.

How it is done

  1. Site: pick the high/dry spot if the site is wet; confirm water table; test hole; stakes; strip topsoil; protect trees/utilities; winter frost protection for open work (ORNL BFH §2.4 SITEWORK). Compact fill and stripped-root zones before footings (AF&PA Details, Foundations).
  2. Termite prep (ongoing): pull roots, stumps, scrap, and form stakes from the foundation area (ORNL §§2.2/3.2/4.2; AF&PA Termites). Soil termiticide where termites are a hazard. Do not disturb treated soil before the slab pour (ORNL checklist). Do not trade treatment for shields.
  3. Footings below local frost, or insulated FPSF if that engineered/IRC alt is chosen (HUD §4.8; ORNL §2.2). Do not pour on frozen ground; do not fill an over-excavated trench; do not bear half-on-rock (ORNL FOOTINGS). Width from soil bearing; equipment buckets 12/16/24 in (HUD §4.4). Thickness ≈ wall thickness and ≈ projection (AF&PA rule of thumb; HUD §4.4: thickness ≥ projection and ≥6 in, projection ≥2 in). Optional two No. 4/No. 5 bars near top. Keyway or dowels to wall (ORNL note 19). Concrete typically 2500 psi (ORNL) or 2500–3000 psi (HUD §4.2); slump 4–6 in; add no water on site.
  4. Walls: CIP or CMU. Local contractor skill and material yards often decide CIP vs CMU more than physics (ORNL §1.3) — keep both in this method. CMU: parge, then dampproof/asphalt; solid cap or bond beam or grouted top course (AF&PA Concrete Foundations; ORNL notes 6, 22, 31, 33). CIP: plug form-tie holes before waterproofing; cure (ORNL checklist). Unreinforced 8-in CIP ≤7 ft fill or 12-in CMU ≤6 ft fill in stable, low-seismic soil (ORNL notes 14, 22); otherwise engineer / IRC tables (HUD §4.5). Wasatch seismic D2 is a reason tables may not be enough — engineer when tables fail, not a stamp.
  5. Dampproof vs waterproof (see waterproof): dampproof + poly for vapor; waterproof if finished basement, poor drainage, or hydrostatic (ORNL §2.2). Waterproof on the wall, insulation outboard of it, down to the footing drain (ORNL note 6).
  6. Subdrain: 4-in perf pipe, holes down, 1 in in 20 ft, gravel envelope, filter fabric, to daylight or sump; clean-out; never connect roof leaders (ORNL note 8). AF&PA: drain tile around the entire CIP/block footing. Not used with PWF.
  7. Basement slab: 4 in min (ORNL note 16; HUD §4.6 ACI 302). 6-mil poly, 12-in laps. Isolation joint at wall; slab floats — do not sit it on a wall ledge (ORNL note 16 / Ch 4 ACI/BRAB). WWF or fibers for shrinkage / radon where called. Cure ~3 days at 70°F (ORNL radon). Soil treatment already done; form wood out of the pour.
  8. Backfill / grade: porous backfill or drainage mat; compact; 5% slope away (6 in in 10 ft); gutters (ORNL notes 2–4). Waterproofing before backfill. Floor framing must exist to brace the top of basement walls before significant backfill (HUD §4.5 simple-span model) — P07 precedes significant basement backfill, can_overlap false.
  9. Sill: treated, ≥8 in above grade, inspectable (ORNL; AF&PA 8 in framing / 6 in siding). Bolts ½ in, ~6 ft o.c., ≤1 ft from corners (ORNL notes 13/31; AF&PA ~6 ft). Embed: follow adopted code; 15 in masonry is the high-wind/uplift variant. Do not pick a winner.

Waterproof vs dampproof — a step, not a type

m-P05-waterproof. Sequence step on CIP/CMU basement (and below-grade crawl faces). Not a competing foundation type.

This is not a foundation type. After the wall is up, coat it before you backfill. Dampproof if you only need vapor; waterproof if the basement will be lived in or the site is wet. Drain tile at the footing for poured or block walls — not for the wood foundation. Protection board over the foam so the backfill does not rip it.

When waterproof beats dampproof: finished basement, poor drainage, water history, or hydrostatic (ORNL BFH §2.2). Dampproof + poly is the vapor-only path.

When this is a bad idea: do not coat then backfill without protection board on exterior foam. Do not put drain tile on PWF. Do not skip this step on a below-grade basement wall. SOG above grade typically needs neither subdrain nor waterproofing (ORNL Ch 4).

How it is done

  1. After the CIP or CMU wall stands: CIP plug form-tie holes; CMU parge first (ORNL checklist; AF&PA Concrete Foundations; ORNL notes 6, 22, 31, 33).
  2. Choose dampproof + poly for vapor vs waterproof if finished basement, poor drainage, water history, or hydrostatic (ORNL BFH §2.2). This is a detailing choice, not a foundation-type winner.
  3. Waterproof on the wall; insulation outboard of it; down to the footing drain (ORNL note 6).
  4. Protection board on exterior foam to 6 in below grade (ORNL note 1).
  5. Drain tile at the CIP/CMU footing (ORNL note 8; AF&PA drain tile around entire CIP/block footing). Tile is NOT used with PWF — do not shop tile onto PWF.
  6. Finish this work before backfill. Waterproofing precedes backfill (ORNL note 1 / §2.2). can_overlap false on that hold.
  7. Do not trade soil treatment for termite shields. Shields optional and not sufficient alone (ORNL).

Fastener schedule: coating sequence, not nails. AF&PA Fig 5 and ORNL Fig 2-8 / notes 1, 6, 8 name waterproof, insulation outboard, protection board, 4-in perf. No page names a fastener size+oc. Do not invent.

Tools: no tool list in sources. Crew count is a Gap. ORNL checklist names construction contractor only.

  • CMU: parge, then dampproof/asphalt (AF&PA). CIP: plug form-tie holes before waterproofing (ORNL).
  • Waterproof on the wall, insulation outboard of it, down to the footing drain (ORNL note 6).
  • Protection board on exterior foam to 6 in below grade (ORNL note 1).
  • Drain tile: 4-in perf, holes down, 1 in in 20 ft (ORNL note 8). NOT PWF.
  • Manufacturer video CLASS: dimple drain membrane / drain tile (not Platon brand; not CIP dampproof-asphalt habit).

Fontana habit — slab on grade

m-P05-slab. Also called slab-on-grade, monolithic thickened-edge slab, stem-wall slab, SOG. Habit South/Southwest (HUD RSDG §4.1). Fontana habit. Integral grade beam where frost is shallow (ORNL §1.3, Ch 4).

Down south and in Fontana the house sits on a slab: either a thickened edge that is the footing, or a short stem wall. Plastic under the slab, no drain tile if the floor is above the dirt. That slab is the first floor — do not order joists. Title 24 may want a thermal break at the edge; that is an insulation detail, not a new foundation type.

When this is the buy: South/Southwest and Fontana. Beats basement when frost is shallow or when ORNL §1.3 water-table veto kills a basement. FPSF is the engineered/IRC path that lets a slab into cold climates without burying the footing — still this method, not a new type. Stem-wall vs monolithic is a D6 split inside this method.

When this is a bad idea: not the Wasatch/Logan 30-in-frost basement habit unless FPSF or frost-depth stem is designed. Expansive soils: 4-in unreinforced habit is a bad idea (HUD §4.6 PTI/ACI 360). Do not shop a P07 joist package on Fontana SOG.

How it is done

  1. Site: compact; strip organics; termite prep including form stakes out of the pour; soil treatment before the slab; do not disturb treated soil (ORNL Ch 4; AF&PA Termites). Do not trade treatment for shields.
  2. Choose stem-wall + spread footing vs monolithic thickened edge (HUD §4.1; ORNL Figs 4-2, 4-3). D6 split: SOG thickened edge is the footing; do not apply the basement float rule as a veto on thickened-edge SOG. Interior bearing: thicken the slab (HUD §4.6).
  3. No subdrain and no waterproofing if the floor is above grade (ORNL Ch 4). High water table: raise the floor and put gravel under the slab.
  4. Build-up: 2–3 in washed gravel or sand + 6-mil poly (HUD §4.6). Gravel is the capillary break. Slab 4 in min (ORNL note 16; HUD §4.6 ACI 302). Elevate slab ≥8 in above grade (ORNL Ch 4).
  5. Isolation at the wall. Do not pour a basement-style shelf for the slab to sit on (ORNL Ch 4 citing ACI/BRAB) — that rule is for basement slabs; SOG thickened edge remains the footing (D6).
  6. WWF or fibers if the slab is >10 ft in a direction (HUD §4.6); ORNL uses WWF for shrinkage/radon. Expansive soils: do not use the 4-in unreinforced habit; PTI/ACI 360 engineered path (HUD §4.6) — engineer, not a new Method from this set.
  7. Edge insulation: exterior vertical foam is the only way to insulate a monolithic grade-beam edge; interior or under-slab foam must break the slab/wall thermal bridge (~R-5 in the joint) (ORNL §4.2). Fontana Title 24 2025 process hook lives here — energy, not a price.
  8. Frost: habit is still bottom of footing below local frost (all three foundation sources). FPSF (foam in lieu of depth) is an engineered/IRC-prescriptive alternate that lets slabs into cold climates (HUD §4.8 ASCE 32-01; ORNL Ch 1/4). Not a separate unsourced method. Foam + termites: IRC/HUD restrict hidden paths unless the structure is termite-resistant.
  9. Sill/anchors on stem or slab edge. This is the first floor in the South — no P07 joist package.

Underslab MEP / radon / termite (P06) happens before the slab pour in the field. Phase spine still P05→P06→P07 as a stub; do not invent lag_days. Sequence hold: soil treatment before slab (ORNL).

Crawl — alt in cold country, habit Northwest and Southeast

m-P05-crawl. Also called crawl-space wall, pier-and-beam crawl, unexcavated crawl. Habit Northwest and Southeast (HUD RSDG §4.1). Alternate in cold climates vs basement. Not the Wasatch/Logan basement habit.

Crawl is the usual foundation in the Northwest and Southeast: short wall or piers, wood kept off the dirt, plastic on the ground. Vents vs no vents is a code call — follow the adopted energy/IRC, do not pick a 1991 or 2001 number yourself. In cold country this is the backup when you do not want a full basement.

When this beats basement: water table within ~8 ft (ORNL §1.3 veto on basement) or when the program does not want a full basement. Piers are the light-touch crawl variant (AF&PA; HUD).

When this is a bad idea: not the Wasatch/Logan basement habit. Vented crawl in a hard-frost climate freezes the space — interior and exterior grade both count (ORNL Ch 3). Do not treat D1 vent ratios as settled. Mapped floodplain: a crawl with interior grade below exterior grade on all sides is classed as a basement (HUD §4.1).

How it is done

  1. Site / termite prep as for CIP/CMU: strip organics; pull roots, stumps, scrap, and form stakes; soil termiticide where hazard; compact before footings (ORNL §2.4 / Ch 3; AF&PA Termites). Do not trade treatment for shields.
  2. Footings below local frost. Vented crawl: interior and exterior grade both count for frost because the space can freeze (ORNL Ch 3).
  3. Walls: CIP, CMU, or piers. AF&PA Other Foundations: free-standing piers commonly 8–12 ft, or piers + curtain walls / grade beams — variants inside this method, not a separate type. HUD §4.1 names pier-and-beam + brick curtain as an economical crawl alternate. Height is what separates crawl vs basement (HUD §4.1).
  4. Dampproof/waterproof generally not required if crawl floor is at or above exterior grade and drainage is adequate (ORNL Ch 3). If the crawl floor is below grade on tight soil, run a basement-like perimeter drain (maybe a sump) — same tile family as CIP/CMU, still not PWF.
  5. Ground cover: 6-mil poly over all exposed soil, 12-in laps, edges to wall (ORNL §3.2 radon/ground cover). AF&PA: 12 in earth-to-girder, 18 in earth-to-joist; treat wood if missed.
  6. Crawl vents vs unvented = D1 variants: AF&PA 1/150 (or 1/1500 with 6-mil + operable louvers); ORNL energy model 1/1500 and prefers unvented/semi-heated but agencies often will not allow year-round closed vents; HUD has no vent-ratio. Follow the adopted IRC/energy code. Do not vent a plenum crawl. Venting is not reliable radon control (ORNL Ch 3).
  7. Posts: AF&PA 1 in above concrete / 6 in above earth vs ORNL 8 in above interior grade. Use the stricter of adopted code vs 8 in when following ORNL.
  8. Grade away 5% (6 in in 10 ft). Treated sill, inspectable. Floor framing (P07) sits on this wall/pier system. Glue T&G and seal penetrations over crawl for air/radon (ORNL §3.2).
  9. Backfill only where walls are below grade; waterproof-before-backfill still applies to that below-grade face. Floor-in-before-significant-backfill (HUD §4.5) if the crawl wall is a stem that needs top brace.

PWF — legal, not the regional default

m-P05-pwf. Also called PWF, permanent wood foundation, treated-wood foundation wall. Alternate in all regions. Model-code and HUD accepted (AF&PA Wood Foundations; ORNL Ch 2 notes 26–30; HUD Fig 4.9 / Table 4.8). Not the regional default. Yards and crews in these three markets are CIP/CMU or SOG.

This is the treated-wood basement or crawl: gravel bed, treated plate and studs, plywood, plastic, gravel to a sump. No drain tile. People pick it to prefab or to keep pouring out of a winter hole. It is legal; it is not what Wasatch or Fontana usually builds. Do not shop it as if there is a public crew.

When this beats habit: winter or prefab schedule (AF&PA). Cold-weather pours are the thing it avoids. Accepted by model codes and HUD as a full system, not a one-off.

When this is a bad idea: not the regional default in Wasatch (basement) or Fontana (slab). Do not add conventional footing drain tile. Do not field-cut untreated walls and call it PWF.

How it is done

  1. Site / termite prep still applies: organics out; soil treatment where hazard; do not trade treatment for shields.
  2. Gravel bed ≥4 in (AF&PA Wood Foundations) / ≥6 in thick and 6 in past the plate (ORNL note 27). Compacted.
  3. Treated footing plate on the stone.
  4. Treated studs + plywood; joints caulked; 6-mil on basement walls (AF&PA; ORNL notes 26–30). This is a treated system per the wood-foundation standard — not a field-cut untreated wall (HUD §4.5 / Fig 4.9).
  5. Gravel under the floor to a center sump (24 in deep, 16 in dia or square) to daylight or pump. No conventional drain tile around a footing — gravel to sump (AF&PA Wood Foundations; ORNL notes 26–30 citing NFPA 1987). Coarse gravel against the lower half of the wall (ORNL).
  6. Slab or treated wood floor over 6-mil (AF&PA; ORNL notes 28–30, 36). PWF may use a treated wood floor in lieu of a basement slab.
  7. Batt in cavities; 2-in air gap above the bottom plate (ORNL note 35).
  8. Winter / prefab is the reason to pick it (AF&PA Wood Foundations).
